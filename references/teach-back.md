# Teach-Back

Use this reference for the final "explain it in your own words" section.

## Teach-Back Goal

The learner should be able to retell the document without copying source wording.

A good teach-back scaffold includes:

- The document's one-sentence thesis.
- The center words.
- The logic or storyline.
- One concrete example or remembered scene/argument from the source, without page-number tags.
- The main caveat or misread risk.
- A final "so what" statement.

## 30-Second Version

Use this template:

```text
这份材料主要讲的是 [central thesis]。
它靠 [2-3 center words] 串起来。
先是 [starting point]，然后 [turn/change]，最后落到 [main implication]。
最容易误读的是 [misread risk]。
所以我会把它理解成 [my words].
```

## 2-Minute Version

Use this structure:

1. One-sentence premise.
2. Three center words and what each does.
3. The logic/storyline in 4-5 beats.
4. One example or remembered scene/argument from the source, without page-number tags.
5. One caveat.
6. Final takeaway.

## 5-Minute Outline

Use this structure:

```text
Opening: why this document matters.
Map: the center words.
Path: how the argument/story/process moves.
Turn: where the reader's understanding changes.
Caveat: what not to overclaim.
Application: how I would use or explain this now.
```

## Self-Check Questions

Ask questions that make the learner produce language:

- Can I explain the document without using its title as the explanation?
- Can I connect the center words in one sentence?
- Can I name the key turn where my understanding changed?
- Can I explain the main caveat without weakening the whole point?
- Can I give one example from the source without copying its wording or reciting page numbers?

## Avoid Saying It This Way

End with one or two weak summaries to avoid:

- "This document is just about X."
- "The author says X is good/bad."
- "The process is simply A then B then C."
- "The character/role is just right/wrong."

Then offer a stronger version:

```text
Better: The document uses X to show how Y changes under condition Z.
```
